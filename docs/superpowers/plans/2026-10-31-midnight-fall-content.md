## 2. The learning check (due in 6 hours)
- Three questions to ask a player **before** they play, on paper or a Google Form:
  1. A sack falls for 2 s. How high was it? (19.6 m)
  2. Drop it from 4× higher. How much longer does it take? (2×)
  3. You push one ball off a table and drop another at the same time. Which lands first? (same time)
- The game asks its own two questions at the end (game plan task 6).

## 3. Playtests (once the case plays end to end, about hour 20)
- At least 3 players, ideally aged 14–16 (siblings, cousins, neighbours); classmates if not.
- For each: the before-score, time spent on each beat in spec §4, where they looked bored or lost, what made them
  smile, their exit-check answers, and the prediction they made at the top balcony.
- Do not help them. Write down what they say out loud.
- Result: one table, sent to Santosh. It becomes a slide: "before X/3, after Y/3".

## 4. The stage
- **Demo script** for spec §6, with who says what and when, timed to 5 minutes.
- **The room prediction**: you ask the audience to guess the top-balcony time before the drop. Practise it.
- **Q&A sheet**: the 10 likeliest judge questions and a two-line answer each. Include "Is death OK for kids?",
  "How do you know they learned?", "What stops the AI from just telling the answer?"
- **Timekeeper** at every rehearsal.
